## Recommended release naming

Prefer descriptive release titles such as:

- Author Focus v4.3 Research Lab — Science-first Word research workflow
- Author Focus Research Lab — Python/Jupyter and Earth Engine research integration
- Word Research Workbench — Citation, integrity and local-AI workflow update

Avoid generic names such as “big update,” “god mode,” or “AI humanizer release.” Descriptive titles are easier for both users and search engines to understand.

## Repository discovery principles

- Keep the repository title and first README paragraph stable and descriptive.
- Put the strongest product description in the first 200–300 words.
- Use real feature language instead of keyword repetition.
- Keep screenshots and captions current.
- Maintain CITATION.cff for scholarly discovery.
- Link to the repository from ORCID, institutional profiles, personal websites, papers, posters, presentations, and relevant project pages where appropriate.
- Use release notes that name the scientific workflow being improved.
- Keep public/private boundaries explicit.
- Do not market AI-detector scores as evidence of authorship or product quality.
- Do not claim a private production feature is open-source unless its implementation is actually published.

## Search intent to optimize for

The best long-term discovery target is not “AI humanizer.” It is the intersection of:

**Microsoft Word + research workflow + citations + scientific integrity + local AI + Python/Jupyter + geospatial analysis.**

That combination better describes the project and attracts researchers, developers, institutions, and research-software collaborators.
